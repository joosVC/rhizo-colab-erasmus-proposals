# KA1 extended concept note — working template

Status: design resource for the future extended-concept skill; not a fifth runnable skill.

## Authoring instructions

Use the selected/refined impact scenario, team decisions and available intake, science and policy reports. Write the concept note in English and discuss missing information in Dutch unless requested otherwise. Draft from existing information; do not turn every output heading below into an intake question.

### Minimum additional capacity input

Ask only for missing information about:

- The realistic number or range of staff and learners the school can involve. Accept ceilings rather than fixed commitments.
- Their profiles: learner age range and educational programme/year; staff roles, teaching subjects or educational responsibilities. Include relevant support needs if known, without collecting personal diagnoses or identifiable participant data.

Suggested Dutch question: “Hoeveel leerlingen en personeelsleden kan de school realistisch laten deelnemen, en welke profielen zijn dat? Denk bij leerlingen aan leeftijd en opleiding/jaar, en bij personeel aan vakken en rollen. Een bandbreedte of maximum volstaat.”

Treat available days, dates, destinations, named hosts/experts, staff release arrangements, detailed resources and budgets as optional at this stage. Use them if supplied. Ask only when an unresolved constraint materially changes the proposed design; otherwise record an open decision or a clearly labelled planning assumption. Do not withhold a useful draft because these details are missing. An activity duration proposed by the author is not confirmed school availability.

Keep applicant identity, field, accreditation status and call year as a separate eligibility check. Retrieve from supplied material when possible. If unresolved, keep the route provisional rather than requiring administrative details before drafting. A 12-month duration does not establish KA122 eligibility. Use a 12-month design horizon as the current RHIZO preference; confirm the applicable call rules before presenting an activity as eligible.

### Design principles

- Build a coherent learning sequence around the agreed need and capacity. Explain how each activity supports a specific objective and how learning returns to school practice.
- Use capacity building and sustainable embedding as the two design pillars. Capacity building may recur; embedding may follow several activities rather than becoming one final event.
- Propose the number and combination of activities needed. Three activities are a useful reference pattern, not a required count. Objectives and activities may have several links; avoid artificial one-to-one mapping.
- Distinguish outgoing learners/staff, accompanying persons, visiting experts and local beneficiaries of an expert visit. A six-person local training team is not six outgoing mobility participants.
- Track participant places across activities separately from unique people. Do not assume recurring participants or resolve contradictory counts silently. Verify formal participant counting with the current rules.
- Describe local transfer and embedding actions without inventing independently funded mobility categories for them. Do not import the KA2 consortium, work-package or numbered-deliverable architecture.
- Use a few concrete learning outcomes per activity. Use SKAV selectively as an authoring aid where helpful, not as four compulsory headings or a long checklist. Describe values as guiding principles in practice, without treating personal beliefs as something to grade.
- Separate learning, evidence of learning, participation and later implementation. Attendance or a recognition document alone does not demonstrate competence. A post-only perception survey supports a report of perceived change, not a measured gain from baseline.
- Keep assumptions, evidence and agreed decisions distinguishable. Apply the same evidence-preserving editorial principles as the impact-scenarios skill: remove stock grant language and inflated claims while retaining numbers, uncertainty, causal limits and traceability.

Aim for a readable discussion document of roughly 4–6 pages, with shorter activity cards where possible. Expand only when the project needs it. Do not apply application-form character limits or write daily schedules in this phase.

## 1. Project at a glance

- Working title and applicant school, if known.
- Provisional action, field, call year and duration; show any unresolved eligibility check.
- Participant capacity and profiles, distinguishing confirmed limits from proposed allocation.
- General objective: the school-level change the project contributes to.
- Impact statement: one clear sentence describing the desired change, for whom, and why it matters, with a suitable horizon and realistic contribution claim.

## 2. School need and the contribution of European learning

Explain the school's main need or challenge using concrete practice examples from the intake. Describe who experiences it and what is already known or remains uncertain.

Explain how learning with schools or experts abroad can help the organisation improve its practice for learners, including those who will not travel. Use relevant scientific and policy findings from the supplied reports; retain source references and limits. Do not present a suspected cause as a settled diagnosis.

## 3. Specific objectives

Repeat a short objective block as needed:

**SO[number] — [desired change]**

- **What should change?** State a concrete, realistic benefit for participants and/or the school.
- **Which need does this address, and how?** Explain the proposed learning and transfer mechanism.
- **How will we recognise success?** Name the principal observable change and a proportionate way of checking it. Give provisional quantities only where justified; leave unsupported thresholds open.

Keep the number of objectives manageable and specific to the selected scenario. Do not impose the four KA2 objective categories.

## 4. Coherent activity sequence

| Activity | Contribution to objective(s) | Participant profile and proposed number | Learning mechanism | Application in school |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A[number] — [title and provisional format] | SO[number(s)] | Within the stated capacity; note repeat participants if known | What participants learn through doing, observing or exchanging | What changes afterwards and how this feeds a later activity |

Explain briefly why the sequence works and which steps depend on earlier learning. Distinguish mobility/expert activities from local preparation, experimentation and embedding. Identify any allocation that still requires a team choice.

## 5. Activity cards

Repeat for each proposed mobility or invited-expert activity. Use the five application-derived questions below as compact authoring prompts, not an additional questionnaire for teachers.

### A[number] — [working title]

**1. What is the planned content, and who takes part?**

Explain why this activity is needed at this point, the participant profiles and proposed numbers, and the practical learning approach. Outline a few meaningful learning blocks rather than a day-by-day itinerary. Give format/duration only as supplied or as a labelled proposal; leave destination open when unknown. Include preparation and support only where they affect the learning design.

**2. What will participants learn?**

Describe a few concrete things participants should understand or be able to do afterwards. Separate staff and learner outcomes where relevant; do not treat accompanying staff as learners automatically. For an invited expert, distinguish the expert's role from the learning outcomes of the local staff/learner group. SKAV may help identify important outcomes but need not appear in the final note.

**3. How will learning be evaluated and recognised?**

Identify a proportionate way to check the principal learning outcomes: a practical task, observed facilitation, peer/expert feedback, reflection or a suitable measurement tool already supported by research. Distinguish self-report from demonstrated performance. Outline recognition appropriate to the activity format and current quality standards; defer detailed instruments and agreements.

**4. Which objective(s) does this support, and what happens next?**

Reference the objective IDs and explain the contribution. Name the first realistic application in school and its link to later activities or embedding. A small transfer artefact can help application; do not require a KA2-style European product.

**5. What host or expert profile is needed?**

Describe the relevant expertise, authentic practice, mentoring or peer-learning environment and ability to support the participant group. Separate known contacts from unconfirmed candidates and missing profiles. Include a search route if already known; do not require a named host, destination or commitment to complete the draft.

For local embedding actions, use a shorter action block in section 6 instead of forcing these five questions onto every team meeting.

## 6. Sustainable use in the school

Describe a few concrete embedding actions, explaining:

- What practice, routine, curriculum element or internal professional learning will continue.
- Which role can carry it, provisionally if not agreed, and what school decision is still needed.
- How colleagues and learners who did not travel will benefit.
- What simple evidence would show that use continues beyond the mobility.

Distinguish embedding from dissemination. Describe internal sharing and any relevant external sharing briefly; publishing materials alone does not establish sustained use. Do not invent a detailed project-management structure or confirmed resources.

## 7. Decisions and evidence to carry forward

End with a short list of decisions that affect the design, plus unresolved eligibility checks. Keep optional logistics in a separate “to confirm later” line. Include references to the source reports so the project writer can recover the evidence.

Do not repeat the entire intake or turn unknowns into an application-completion checklist. Prioritise only questions that must be resolved for the next team discussion.

## Design provenance and source checks

This structure was informed by RHIZO3's Call 2026 KA122-SCH submitted application: project objectives (pp. 9–13), activity descriptions (pp. 16–25), and follow-up/embedding (pp. 37–38), checked against the accompanying working document. Retain the useful question structure, not the project's private content, assumptions, target values or partner choices. The example contains inconsistent staff counts and uses a different project duration; it is a design reference, not a compliance authority or a source of default quantities.

The five activity prompts above paraphrase the application structure and add an explicit transfer link for this early design stage. They do not reproduce every mandatory application field. Use the applicable current form for a full application.

Verify formal requirements against the relevant call-year Programme Guide and EPOS material:

- [School education mobility](https://erasmus-plus.ec.europa.eu/programme-guide/part-b/key-action-1/school-pupils-and-staff).
- [VET mobility](https://erasmus-plus.ec.europa.eu/programme-guide/part-b/key-action-1/mobility-vet).
- [EPOS KA1 library](https://www.epos-vlaanderen.be/bibliotheek-categorie/erasmus-ka1/): use materials for the correct field, action and year.

Do not infer future-call eligibility from this design template or transplant school-pupil activity questions to VET without checking its formats.
