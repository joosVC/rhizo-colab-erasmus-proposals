# Capacity-led KA1 learning and transfer

## Minimum capacity conversation

Reuse known participant numbers/ranges and profiles. Ask only what is missing: how many learners/staff the school can realistically involve, learner ages and educational programmes/years, staff subjects/roles. A ceiling is enough. Do not make availability in days, countries, budgets, named hosts or staffing logistics a prerequisite. An unconfirmed capacity range can remain an open design choice.

Suggested question: “Hoeveel leerlingen en personeelsleden kan de school realistisch laten deelnemen, en welke profielen zijn dat? Bij leerlingen: leeftijd en opleiding/jaar. Bij personeel: vakken en rollen. Een bandbreedte of maximum volstaat.”

Propose how the available capacity might be used in service of the selected need; do not fill mobility places without a learning purpose. Use the current RHIZO preference of a 12-month horizon as an explicit proposal unless another duration is agreed. Dates and activity durations may be open or proposed without implying school availability is confirmed.

## Two design pillars

Capacity building can involve staff learning, pupil learning and invited expertise. Repetition is useful when it represents purposeful practice or a staged learning sequence. Explain what each group learns and why international interaction is needed.

Embedding means applying learning in school routines, curriculum, team learning and quality work, with proposed owners and decisions. It can run throughout. Internal sharing is useful but does not itself establish use; a concrete first application and means of maintaining it are needed. Do not create invented funding categories for local implementation actions.

Use an activity sequence tailored to the context. The RHIZO3 example used invited expertise, pupil group mobility and job shadowing. These are examples, not a default menu to copy or a compulsory three-activity structure. Another school may need only staff learning and local transfer. An expert visit alone does not meet the 2026 KA122 requirement for at least one staff or learner mobility.

## Activity descriptions

Use the five compact questions in the KA1 template: content/profiles, learning, evaluation/recognition, SO contribution/transfer, and host/expert profile. Draft from the reports and chosen scenario; ask the teacher only about consequential gaps. Use a few learning blocks, not daily schedules or a travel itinerary. Keep cultural learning purposeful rather than importing a fixed work/culture ratio from an old example.

Use a few specific learning outcomes that fit the age, programme and roles. SKAV may help author them: skills are capabilities developed through knowledge/practice; knowledge is understanding; attitudes concern dispositions/readiness for action; values concern guiding principles expressed in professional conduct. Do not force four lists, grade personal beliefs or import outcome claims for every accompanying person. For invited expertise, distinguish the visiting expert from the local group that learns.

Recognise learning using the appropriate activity-format documentation. Do not mandate individual learning agreements or Europass certificates for every group participant because one old application did so. Keep practical evaluation distinct from recognition paperwork and self-perceived learning distinct from demonstrated capability.

## Capacity and route checks

Distinguish the school capacity envelope, participant places by activity, unique participants, accompanying persons, expert visitors and local beneficiaries. Use overlap only if stated or clearly proposed; do not silently assume all teachers are the same people. Flag contradictory counts. Accompanying teachers are not automatically staff-mobility learners.

Use the current rules for each proposed format and field; school education and VET are not interchangeable. Check whether learner ages/programmes fit the action and hosting context. A university or company hosting staff does not convert the sending school's educational field.

Check the actual applicant/OID and relevant-field accreditation rather than assuming the whole RHIZO group shares one status. A known same-field accreditation rules out KA122 under the 2026 baseline. Retain a useful learning design with the funding route unresolved/corrected for discussion; do not automatically switch to KA121 or apply KA122 ceilings to an accredited project. HED requires a separate framework; a provisional pedagogical outline may still be useful if requested.

Where capacity exceeds a formal limit, show the conflict and a concrete eligible-scale proposal, explicitly requiring a team decision. Do not silently lower the agreed capacity, split applications to evade rules or count accompanying persons as learners. Separate an early conceptual design from a fully verified application.
