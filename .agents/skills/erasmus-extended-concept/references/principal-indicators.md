# Principal changes and indicative quantities

## Keep the concept-level scope

For KA2 prioritise the main qualitative change associated with each R-numbered deliverable. Normally give one principal qualitative change and one meaningful quantitative reach/use measure, adding a second only when it describes a genuinely distinct priority. The point is a few useful indicators, not a full register or a long series of subdeliverables.

Separate the output, activity, intended outcome, indicator, target and measurement direction. A kit is an output; training is an activity; competent use is an outcome; observed appropriate use is an indicator. “Thirty teachers attended” establishes reach, not competent use. Qualitative change can later be scored quantitatively; qualitative does not mean unmeasurable.

For each result capture briefly:

- Who should change and what they should do/experience differently after using it.
- A suitable construct or scientific insight, if available in the supplied research.
- A feasible measurement direction, and a named scale/tool only when supported and sufficiently suitable for the population, language and use.
- A principal reach/use quantity and its basis, or an explicitly open target.

Use a supported, feasible scale if helpful; do not require one. If the research offers no suitable construct or tool, describe the observable change in plain language. Do not invent a scale, claim validation from a prior AI chat or prescribe a clinical instrument based only on its title. Mark candidate tools as candidates until suitability is checked. Do not force SKAV into KA2.

For KA1 use the few learning outcomes and school-transfer changes in the activity cards. Evidence could include a practical demonstration, a reviewed application plan or observed use after return. Recognising participation/learning and showing subsequent school change are different tasks.

## Quantitative planning heuristics

RHIZO's indicative school-piloting pattern is 6–10 teachers trained per implementing partner/site, of whom about 3–5 pilot with roughly 20 pupils each: around 60–100 pupils per implementing partner. Treat these as proposed experience-based ranges, not scientific findings, mandatory training-session counts or EU requirements. Confirm a different meaning if the user says “trainings” rather than teachers. Use actual supplied capacities first.

Count only sites that really implement. Include a core school if it pilots; do not multiply pupil reach by a university or a network partner that only develops/disseminates. If there are 3 implementing sites, the illustrative total is 18–30 teachers trained, 9–15 piloting teachers and 180–300 pupil places before checking overlap. Distinguish unique pupils from repeated sessions or shared classes; do not promise unique reach from multiplication alone.

Do not automatically apply pilot reach to every result and sum it again across the portfolio. Use a shared cohort where appropriate and cross-reference it. Knowledge development or a sustainability resource may need a different use measure; do not force a teacher pilot onto every output. Downloads and impressions describe exposure, not adoption.

Explain whether each number comes from an agreed limit, a supplied example, RHIZO's heuristic, an author proposal or an actual finding. Keep contradictory sample counts unresolved until clarified. Targets in old impact sheets are project commitments, not effect-size evidence or universal capacity norms; copied cells and version conflicts require checking.

## Qualitative target discipline

Do not invent percentage gains, starting scores or baselines. Describe the intended observable direction first. If the user supplied a target, retain its proposed status and note what validation it needs. A future 70% self-report target is not an observed 70% increase in competence. Retain denominators, timing and perceived-versus-demonstrated distinctions.

Use proportionate RACER checks adapted from the Commission handbook: relevant to the objective, acceptable to the partners, credible/verifiable, easy to collect, and robust against manipulation. Apply these as author checks, not five extra output columns. The extended note need not specify complete survey items, sampling plans, psychometrics, data governance, an evaluation budget or every indicator's collection frequency. Those belong to later design.

Show a short measurement direction and an appropriate assessment point such as after supported practice or during follow-up. Make longer-term benefit a contribution claim; do not promise causal attribution that the design cannot support.
