## Dutch decision brief

In 100–180 words, explain the proposed architecture, the most important change, complementary expertise and what the team should decide. Keep the English concept self-contained too.

## 1. Project frame and intended impact

State proposed/agreed action, educational field, call year/baseline, applicant, lump sum and duration when known. Show unresolved eligibility/version questions briefly.

Start the impact account with “Our desired impact is…” or an equally direct sentence. State one general objective, then a concise impact statement about the change for defined beneficiaries and its value. Explain the selected need, current practice and why cooperation adds something useful. Synthesize the supporting science and policy with source IDs and the main causal assumption; avoid duplicating whole reports.

## 4. Result portfolio and principal changes

Use a small number of R-numbered shareable deliverables, normally one led by each core knowledge organisation. Repeat a compact card:

### R[number] — [major deliverable]

- **Purpose and users:** the product, its major components and how it supports SO[number(s)] / WP or cluster.
- **Lead and collaboration:** proposed accountable profile/partner, complementary contributors and what testing/revision is needed.
- **Expected qualitative change and principal indicator:** the main change in users/practice, how using this result could contribute, and the observable signal that would indicate it.
- **Measurement direction:** supported construct/tool if appropriate, otherwise an observable change and feasible assessment direction. Keep effect targets open unless justified.
- **Indicative quantity and basis:** principal reach/use range, cohort overlap and whether it is agreed, a heuristic or proposed. Reuse shared cohort assumptions rather than counting the same people anew per result.

Do not number kit components, training events, pilots, management or dissemination channels as additional results. Note a justified exception to the normal lead-result/core-partner alignment.

## 5. Sustained use, transfer and dissemination

Describe who will use the results, support needed to adopt them, proposed continuing ownership and integration into normal practice. Explain the network's contribution and conditions for transfer. Distinguish access, reach, adoption and repeated use. Describe SO4's audiences/channels without inventing an extra numbered product.

## 6. Priority decisions and source annex

Keep this section and the annex in English, like the rest of the concept. List at most five consequential team decisions with a concrete proposal where possible. Keep later checks separate. Do not disguise a missing report as established evidence or a 2026 baseline as confirmed 2027 eligibility.
