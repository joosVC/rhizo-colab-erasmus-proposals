# [Working title] — KA1 extended concept note

Authoring template: use supplied context and capacity to draft a readable proposal. Do not ask every prompt below to the teacher. Remove instructional prompts from the final output; retain open decisions and proposal status.

## Dutch decision brief

Explain the proposed learning sequence, intended school change, use of available participant capacity and priority choices in 100–180 words.

## 1. Project at a glance and intended impact

State applicant, educational field, target call and provisional action when known, with eligibility gaps visible. Use a proposed 12-month horizon unless another duration is agreed. Give participant capacity/ranges and profiles, including learner age/programme and staff roles/subjects. Keep proposed allocations distinct from confirmed limits.

State one general objective and a separate concise impact statement: the expected change, for whom and why it matters, with a suitable horizon and contribution claim.

## 2. School need and European learning contribution

Explain the main school need using concrete intake examples. Explain how international learning can improve the school for learners, including those who do not travel. Use the relevant science/policy findings and source limits. Do not convert suspected causes into established diagnoses.

## 3. Specific objectives

Repeat a short block as needed:

**SO[number] — [desired change]**

- **What should change?** State a concrete benefit for participants and/or school practice.
- **Which need does this address, and how?** Explain the learning and transfer mechanism.
- **How will we recognise success?** Name the principal observable change and a proportionate assessment direction; keep unsupported targets open.

Use as many objectives as the design needs. Do not impose KA2's four functions or an artificial one-to-one relation between objectives and activities.

## 4. Coherent activity sequence

| Activity / proposed format | SO contribution | Participant profile and allocation | Learning mechanism | First application in school |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A[number] — [title] | SO[number(s)] | [numbers/ranges; distinguish overlap, outgoing participants, accompanying persons and local beneficiaries] | [what is learned through doing, observing or exchange] | [how learning feeds practice or a later activity] |

Explain the sequence and dependencies briefly. Separate funded activity formats from local preparation, experimentation and embedding. Do not force three mobilities, a detailed itinerary or a KA2 product portfolio.

## 5. Activity cards

Repeat for each proposed mobility or invited-expert activity. Use a few meaningful learning blocks, not a daily schedule. Give durations/destinations only when supplied or clearly proposed; they are not required intake fields.

### A[number] — [working title]

**Content and participant profiles.** Why this activity at this point? What will participants do and with whom? How does the content fit learner ages/programmes or staff roles? Distinguish an invited expert from the local group learning with them. Mention relevant support where it changes the learning design.

**Expected learning.** State a few concrete things participants should understand or be able to do. Separate learner and staff outcomes when relevant. Use SKAV selectively if helpful, without compulsory lists or claims that all accompanying staff have staff-learning objectives.

**Evaluation and recognition.** Describe a proportionate way of assessing the main learning, distinguishing perception from demonstrated performance. Outline appropriate activity-format recognition; defer full instruments and agreements.

**Objective contribution and transfer.** Reference the SO IDs, causal contribution and first realistic application in school. Explain how this prepares a later step or sustained use. A small transfer artefact may help; do not require a shareable European product for every activity.

**Host or expert profile.** Describe required expertise, authentic practice, mentoring or peer environment. Distinguish known contacts from unconfirmed candidates and missing profiles. Named hosts/countries are optional at this stage; do not invent commitments.

## 6. Sustainable use in school

Describe a few embedding actions rather than five-question cards for every local meeting. State what practice, routine, curriculum element or internal professional learning could continue; the proposed responsible role and decision needed; benefits for non-travelling colleagues/learners; and simple evidence of continued use. Distinguish embedding from internal/external sharing. Do not invent approved resources or local mobility-funding categories.

## 7. Decisions and evidence to carry forward

List at most five consequential team choices and any decisive route/eligibility conflict. Keep optional logistics as “to confirm later”. Include compact references to source reports and actual rule-check coverage. Do not turn this ending into a full application-completion checklist or block a useful draft because days and destinations remain open.
