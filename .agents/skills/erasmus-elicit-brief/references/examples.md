# Example framings

These examples are synthetic. Adapt the reasoning, not their school facts or outcomes.

## A broad diversity question

Input: “Our classes are increasingly diverse. During group work a few pupils dominate while others withdraw. We do not know whether language, belonging, task design or something else explains it.”

Frame: “What mechanisms and established or emerging classroom approaches are relevant to unequal participation in group work in diverse secondary classrooms, and what evidence supports them?” Explore candidate lenses such as classroom interaction, belonging and group-task design because each may help explain participation. Do not turn suspected language barriers into a confirmed cause or replace participation with an unrelated inclusion programme. Ask the report to distinguish dimensions and measures of participation.

## A surfing preference

Input: “We already run water sports as sport days. We wonder whether surfing could support pupils who have lost learning motivation. Effects are unknown.”

Frame evidence for surfing/water activities in relation to the supplied intended change, with comparable outdoor/group activities and transfer from informal settings explicitly labelled. If wellbeing is also mentioned, distinguish wellbeing outcomes from re-engagement with learning. Do not infer that improved wellbeing proves improved attendance or motivation. Retain surfing as a legitimate research candidate; do not dismiss it for being niche.

## AI literacy

Input: “Secondary pupils use AI-generated answers but teachers are unsure how to help them judge those answers. They want approaches to verification and critical judgement.”

Explore AI literacy with candidate adjacent lenses in critical evaluation, epistemic judgement or metacognition; explain their connection. Ask for definitions, actual assessed outcomes, and recent developments alongside foundations. Do not assert that a fashionable named method is scientifically superior, force a policy framework or equate confident tool use with critical judgement.

## Environmental citizenship

Input: “Pupils know environmental facts but teachers see little collective action. They want to understand what helps pupils act together.”

Explore environmental citizenship with candidate lenses in collective agency, action competence and place-based learning. Ask whether research distinguishes knowledge, attitudes, intentions and observed action. Treat combinations as possibilities to evaluate, not an already validated intervention package. Do not assume climate anxiety, low motivation or school-wide consensus unless supplied.

## A sparse note

Input: “We want more inclusion. We have no concrete examples yet. Write the prompt now.”

Produce an exploratory question distinguishing meanings, barriers and responses within the educational setting that was actually stated. Mark unspecified population/context and avoid invented ages or local causes. Explain that this scan can guide a later conversation rather than identify the school's actual need. Do not force a new interview or a shortlist of unsupported solutions.
