# Elicit prompt template

Adapt this template into one self-contained prompt. Replace bracketed fields using actual input, label unknowns, remove unused alternatives and shorten repetition. Do not insert research conclusions or a predetermined shortlist. The context and main question must still make sense without the original startnotitie.

## Research question

[One neutral question about relevant approaches/mechanisms, evidence for a preferred solution, or distinctions within a broad educational need. Include the supplied population, context and intended change where known.]

## Context and purpose

We are exploring possible directions for an educational cooperation project, before choosing a solution or writing a concept note. [Brief non-identifying account of the reported school experience.] [Separate the preferred solution and suspected explanation from observations.] [State consequential unknowns.] These are starting hypotheses and local accounts, not established scientific findings. Produce an English scientific landscape report that supports later choices, rather than selecting a final intervention or designing the project.

## Questions to investigate

1. [Clarify relevant constructs, challenges and mechanisms, or assess the preferred intervention against plausible alternatives.]
2. Which established and emerging approaches address [the challenge/change], for whom, under which conditions, and with what supporting or conflicting evidence?
3. [User-supplied focused question, or implementation/transfer question necessary for this case.]
4. [Optional remaining focused question; remove if redundant.]

Explore [up to three relevant adjacent lenses] because [explicit connections to the school question]. Treat these as candidate search directions: refine or discard them if their educational relevance or evidence is insufficient.

## Evidence and scope

Prioritise [actual population/setting]. Include international evidence and identify contextual relevance; separate direct school evidence from findings transferred from other populations/settings. Retain foundational research and current syntheses, and examine developments in the preceding five years for emerging approaches without excluding older relevant studies. Include quantitative, qualitative, mixed-methods and implementation evidence as appropriate. Distinguish evaluated interventions from conceptual proposals, research methods and preprints. Seek null, negative and contradictory findings alongside positive results. Do not assume one best approach or infer effects from theoretical plausibility alone.

## Requested synthesis

Map the relevant landscape, then compare the most useful directions in a readable table: concept/type; intended change and mechanism; study designs/populations/settings and key findings; maturity and confidence with reasons; limitations; school applicability and implementation conditions; equity/access implications; and a possible adaptation/development/testing direction. Attribute substantive claims to sources and mark unavailable details as unknown. Distinguish measured outcomes from suggested measures and reported conditions from design hypotheses.

Include promising emerging approaches with limited evidence when relevant, clearly labelled; distinguish these from established evidence. Report studied uptake or practice gaps only with sources and scope. Do not infer that an approach is rarely implemented merely because evidence is sparse, or claim novelty in Erasmus+.

Offer an evidence-linked shortlist of distinct directions for later discussion, normally two to five if supported; do not fill a quota. Explain why each merits consideration and what remains to validate in our context. Suggest combinations only where mechanisms plausibly complement each other, distinguishing evaluated combinations from untested design possibilities. Briefly explain why weakly relevant or unsupported directions were set aside.

Provide references with author/year/title and DOI or stable links, and a concise account of the actual search date, coverage, selection process and limitations where available. Distinguish insufficient evidence from evidence of no effect. Do not fabricate citations, counts, causal certainty or systematic-review completeness. Exclude EU policy/priority screening, PRP searches, partner selection, funding recommendations and a Theory of Change.
