# KA1 mobility design

Use the same impact levels with a learning-and-transfer mechanism. Keep the sequence pedagogically coherent. Do not merely list visits or require the KA2 product portfolio.

## Link objectives to a learning sequence

For each specific objective, identify whose capability or practice should change, what relevant learning is needed, why international experience adds value, and how that learning will be applied.

Map:
- Preparation: identify learning questions, readiness, access needs, participants and relevant observation/practice tasks.
- Mobility: propose a purposeful format such as job shadowing, training or learner mobility when appropriate; keep formal format eligibility unverified until the action rules are checked.
- Reflection: capture and interpret what participants learned, with feedback or assessment appropriate to the objective.
- Transfer: test/adapt the learning locally, support colleagues and gather evidence of application.
- Embedding: establish ownership, time and routines that enable sustained use.

A sequence may involve multiple mobilities or groups, but do not invent destinations, hosts, participant counts or a schedule. Explain the connection between phases rather than treating each visit as a self-contained project.

## Retain outputs and outcomes

Mobility still has outputs: completed learning experiences, participant learning records, shared practices or an agreed implementation approach. Distinguish these from demonstrable competence, classroom application and organisational change.

A pupil exchange can have meaningful learning outcomes, not only travel/intercultural exposure. Describe intended learning, preparation, appropriate reflection and transfer in relation to the actual need. Do not assume every staff mobility directly produces improved learner wellbeing.

Use observation ideas such as a documented adaptation applied in lessons, feedback on participant learning or changes in an agreed school routine. Leave baseline, targets and exact follow-up timing open.

## Design for the organisation

Ask which school processes could embed the learning and who must enable them. Propose proportionate actions such as a supported classroom trial, co-planning with colleagues, integration in staff development or curriculum review. Disseminating a travel report alone does not establish sustained practice change.

Recognise access and participation barriers, workload, follow-up support and staff continuity. Do not make school-wide endorsement or an approved internationalisation strategy a universal condition to start discussing a provisional scenario.

## Preserve scope

Support scenario design for a supplied KA1 context. Do not select accreditation versus short-term action, claim eligibility, calculate grants or draft an Erasmus Plan/application. The core design can be used across fields, but formats, actors and formal rules remain domain/action-specific. Retain these limitations visibly when inputs are sparse.
