# KA2 cooperation design

Use the RHIZO pattern as a design aid. Choose the needed functions before choosing product formats. Do not reproduce a standard handbook/training/MOOC list in every dossier.

## Use the three functions flexibly

| Function | Change to explore | Possible result formats | Conditions to examine |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Knowledge development and sharing | Partners/professionals develop a shared understanding or school-usable approach | Framework, handbook, protocols, cases, design principles | Complementary expertise, local relevance, co-design and testing |
| Capacity building and application | Teachers can apply/adapt/evaluate the approach; learners experience the intended change | Training package, lesson resources, coaching tools, evaluation kit, pilot learnings | Guided practice, feedback, opportunities and time to apply |
| Sustained use and wider uptake | Participating organisations continue use; others can adopt it under stated conditions | Implementation playbook, peer network, accessible course, policy brief | Ownership, updating, organisational mandate, support and resources |

Knowledge sharing may itself build capacity; do not force artificial separate objectives or WPs when the same mechanism serves both. A pilot can contribute to several functions. Distinguish change in the project team, change in participating teachers and change in learners.

For a novel model, propose evidence-informed development and feasibility testing; do not call an untested combined model validated. For an existing practice, consider adaptation, transfer and implementation before inventing a new product. Research may reveal that a cooperation project is premature.

## Map objectives to results

Use stable IDs within a scenario (SO1, SO2; R1, R2; A1, A2). A readable chain might link SO1 “Teachers can use a shared approach” to R1 “Practice framework” and R2 “Training/support,” plus co-design, training and classroom application. Explain the mechanism; do not replace it with IDs.

Map every substantive objective to one or more results and supporting activities. Each result must have a prospective user and use case. In later design these can become WP candidates. Identify omitted/merged functions explicitly rather than treating the standard pattern as obligatory.

Treat management, coordination and quality assurance as cross-cutting enablers. Do not add a standalone pedagogical objective for WP1. A genuine organisational-capacity objective is possible when it addresses the problem, not merely because management exists.

Keep KA210 activity design distinct from KA220 implementation WPs. Do not assign a full formal WP architecture, budget percentages or compulsory outputs at this stage. For HED, do not suggest KA210-HED as an assumed eligible route; defer formal eligibility to the relevant call scan.

## Explain why cooperation matters

Name the kinds of expertise to combine and the two-way learning/translation benefit. State what cross-context comparison, co-development or transfer adds beyond local work. Do not invent named partners, organisations' willingness, existing networks or an assured consortium.

For example, practice expertise in an informal setting and a school's pedagogical/curricular expertise might jointly support a transfer hypothesis. The transfer still needs testing; expertise exchange alone does not prove an effect.

## Design sustainability substantively

A course remains available only if maintained; availability does not establish continued use. A network needs a purpose, facilitator, participation incentives and resources. A policy brief requires appropriate audiences and a plausible influence pathway; do not guarantee adoption.

Distinguish internal embedding from external uptake. Propose who should own implementation and which routines could support it (curriculum planning, professional development, induction, scheduled peer learning). Mark mandate and resource commitments unconfirmed.

Evaluate possible burden and exclusion: attendance barriers, staff turnover, accessibility, dependence on specialist infrastructure or expertise, and whether other schools can reasonably adapt the approach. Scale is a conditional design possibility, not an automatic property of a digital product.
