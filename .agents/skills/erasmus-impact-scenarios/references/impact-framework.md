# Impact Tool design rules

Use the National Agency tools' common vocabulary as a readable interface. Preserve the distinction between changes and things delivered. This is a RHIZO adaptation for early design, not a replica of the official form or a requirement to complete it.

## Map the levels

| Level | Design question | RHIZO use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Desired impact | What broader improvement should the project contribute to? | Overall ambition, beneficiaries and contribution limits |
| Outcomes | What changes in people's capability, behaviour or organisational practice? | Specific objectives |
| Outputs | What concrete things or completed learning experiences support those changes? | Results/deliverables |
| Activities | What work produces, tests and helps people use those outputs? | Development, exchange, training, mobility, application |
| Inputs | What expertise, time, support and resources make it possible? | Enabling needs, not a fabricated budget |

Use backward design from impact; check the resulting causal chain forward. Describe staff, learners, organisation and relevant external users when supported. Identify direct and indirect beneficiaries; leave unsupported levels open rather than inventing an outcome for every group.

The official worksheet separates staff, learners/young people and organisation; it also provides spaces for monitoring and stakeholder involvement. In an early scenario, use the audience distinction and note likely measurement/stakeholder needs without requiring all four worksheet pages to be complete.

## Keep objectives and outputs distinct

Prefer “Teachers can adapt and apply X in their lessons” over “Develop an X handbook.” Treat publication, delivered training and completed mobility as outputs; distinguish these from gained competence, changed practice and learner benefit. A deliverable can support several objectives, and an objective may need several deliverables/activities. Use a primary link for readability, with supporting links where necessary.

Make a causal bridge concrete: “Through guided practice, feedback and repeated classroom trials, teachers become able to adapt the approach; this may improve learner participation if the barriers identified locally are actually addressed.” Mark the training mechanism, transfer and learner link according to their different evidence strength.

## Keep early design honest

Use Proposed, To decide, Reported, Supported in supplied report, Transfer hypothesis and Unknown where useful. Mark “not yet investigated” differently from “investigated but inconclusive.” Leave quantities, effect sizes and targets unset unless supplied and attributable. Allow one solid route or a research-first conclusion.

Explain conditions and rival explanations at weak links. Distinguish a necessary enabling condition from a confirmed local resource. Do not assume continued funding, workload capacity, leadership mandates or learner buy-in.

Use short-term and longer-term expectations, with timing to agree. Do not turn the tool's illustrative six-month outcome wording into a universal deadline. Describe broader impact as a contribution: avoid claiming sole attribution or declaring that impact can never be observed.

## Check result use

Ask who uses each result, in which practice, with what support and why they would continue. Distinguish:
- Reach: people hear about or obtain a resource.
- Adoption: people try/use it in their practice.
- Embedding: an organisation maintains and supports routine use.

A download or training attendance count can track an output/reach; it cannot alone demonstrate changed teaching or pupil wellbeing. An early observation idea might be an observed lesson adaptation, teacher reflection triangulated with practice, learner accounts or an organisational decision, with method/ethics/baseline still to design.

## Keep the national tool's role clear

Treat the tools as Erasmus-specific guidance. Keep actual call rules in the supplied policy scan or a selectively verified official action passage. Do not derive grant points, mandatory objectives, WP numbers or action eligibility from the worksheet or its examples.
