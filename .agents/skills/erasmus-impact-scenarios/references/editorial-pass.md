# Edit Erasmus prose without changing the evidence

Apply this pass to the completed Dutch discussion brief and English scenario prose. Use it again for future concept-writing outputs. Match an explicit writing sample where it improves readability; keep grant and research prose neutral, specific and suitable for teachers and project writers.

This is an embedded RHIZO adaptation of [blader/humanizer](https://github.com/blader/humanizer/blob/225a6f39ac85f76ee48dbad772ea4abe4ed6c9d8/SKILL.md), version 3.1.0, inspected on 2026-10-14. The upstream project is MIT licensed; retain [its licence](humanizer-license.txt) with this adaptation. No external installation, paid service, runtime script or live upstream fetch is required to apply this pass.

## Freeze the content first

Keep the authoritative draft and its claims while editing prose. Preserve:
- Facts, figures, dates, percentages, denominators, baseline/target distinctions and measurement timing.
- People, organisations, beneficiaries, commitments and proposed versus confirmed status.
- Scenario, objective, result, activity and WP identifiers, their mappings and responsibilities.
- Source links, citations, locators, consultation coverage and evidence confidence.
- Negation, causal direction, contribution versus attribution, transfer assumptions and uncertainty.
- Domain/action, national context and target versus baseline call year.
- Domain terminology and genuine categories, including SKAV when used.

Do not fill information gaps with a plausible detail, invent a first-person experience or add a reaction on behalf of RHIZO. Do not turn expected change into achieved change or perceived improvement into demonstrated improvement. Do not rename required section headings, variables or technical terms just to vary words.

## Edit the prose

| Pattern | Editorial action | Preserve when meaningful |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Staged openers and chatbot wrappers | Start with the finding, proposal or decision; remove praise, offers and explanations of what the document is about to do | An actual question or necessary handoff |
| Empty contrasts and invented objections | State the positive claim directly | A contrast that corrects a documented misconception or explains a real trade-off |
| Dramatic closers and repeated summaries | End on the last useful finding or decision; remove repeated lessons and slogans | A new consequence the reader needs |
| Forced triads and mechanical parallelism | Use the number of distinct ideas supported; combine repetition and vary sentence structure | Real sets of three, four SKAV dimensions, or objective/result structures |
| Inflated significance and stock grant language | Name the actor, action and expected change; replace unsupported superlatives and abstract claims | Official priority names, necessary terms and attributed quotations |
| Vague authority and loose connections | Keep the actual consulted source and the relationship it supports; move detailed provenance to the ledger when appropriate | Missing-source notices that limit the conclusion |
| Decorative formatting and punctuation | Use sentence-case headings, restrained emphasis and short sentences; replace decorative dashes or arrows | Tables, IDs, quotations, source titles, meaningful ranges and causal notation |
| Stacked qualifiers and passive vagueness | Keep the shortest accurate qualification and name the actor when known | A real limitation, unknown actor or conditional implementation assumption |
| Re-explaining shared context | Keep a self-contained team brief, but remove duplicated background across cards | Context an absent team member needs and the caveats beside affected claims |

Apply the same principles in Dutch and English. Prefer concrete wording over stock phrases such as “unlock transformative potential,” “foster a robust ecosystem,” or “een baanbrekende, holistische aanpak.” Avoid decorative contractions, deliberate errors or casual anecdotes as a way to sound human. Natural professional prose can include technical vocabulary and necessary uncertainty.

Treat a word or punctuation mark as a cue to review, not an automatic deletion rule. A literal quoted phrase, proper name, priority title, required structure or genuine causal contrast can remain. A list of deliverables can legitimately contain several items; do not delete one to break a rhythm.

## Check the edited version against the draft

Compare sentence meaning and table entries, not only words. Check each number/date/name/ID/source link and every consequential caveat. Confirm that causal strength, observation versus hypothesis, participant counts, perceived versus observed change, future-year limits and ownership status survived. Restore any loss before delivery.

Ask whether a teacher or project writer can identify who changes, how, under which conditions and what the team must decide. Keep length within the core budget. A clearer sentence must remain equally accurate.

Return only the finished discussion pack in normal use. Keep draft, critique and editorial reasoning internal unless the user requests them. Do not claim that the result is guaranteed to pass an AI detector, was written by a person, or is now more scientifically substantiated. This pass improves writing; it supplies no new evidence.
