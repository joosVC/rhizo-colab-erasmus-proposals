# Research and fit judgments

## Keep the need central

Extract the supplied challenge, affected learners/staff, educational context, intended change and uncertainties. Retain the participant's preferred solution without treating its efficacy as established. Use scientific directions from a supplied Elicit report as optional search lenses, preserving its limitations and citations as leads to verify when needed. Do not require research completion before mapping policy relevance.

Distinguish the sector receiving the main intended impact from partners' organisation types. Cross-sector involvement may help; it does not make every sector priority applicable. Compare SCH, VET and HED when genuinely open, using separate rows. Read action scope before advice: in the 2026 baseline KA210 is not a higher-education field route, even though HEIs may participate in eligible small-scale partnerships under the stated conditions. Reverify for a new year. This is a route guardrail, not a full eligibility audit.

## Bounded search rounds

1. Check the call baseline and agency relevance, then draft two to four themes from the input. Include one adjacent theme only where it can explain the need or add a credible policy contribution.
2. Search primary institutional sources across those themes and the date window. Use dates in the queries and recency filters for discovery, then verify the actual document dates. Follow cited foundational policies beyond the window where needed.
3. Follow the strongest leads to original texts, data and implementation evidence. Make one gap-filling pass for important missing sectors, conflicting claims or announced initiatives whose status may have changed.

Normally aim for about six to twelve substantive documents, including baseline sources, but treat that as a planning range rather than a quota. Stop when the main question and promising connections are supported and consequential gaps have been resolved or disclosed. For a quick scan, narrow depth and label coverage. Do not repeat failing searches endlessly or claim full horizon monitoring. Report a bounded negative finding, e.g. “No additional directly relevant adopted policy was found in the sources searched”, not “There is no policy”.

## Status and momentum

Compare older foundations with developments inside the window. Identify what is actually new: adoption, implementation step, new evidence, revised guidance, consultation or political agenda. Page updates and reposts are not automatically policy change. Publication of a report using 2024 data in 2026 is new evidence publication, not a 2026 measurement.

Strong momentum requires a relevant documented action or explicit institutional follow-through; political announcements may support a conditional opportunity. Count a repeated press release and its source as one development. Do not interpret “planned for November” as already adopted or “will be wanted in next year's call”. Where important, recheck whether a planned initiative has subsequently been adopted.

An implementation gap must be documented or described as a local/research question. A newly published framework is not proof that no classroom resources exist; inspect existing resources. A practical adaptation opportunity may still exist for a specific context. Novelty within Erasmus+ is left for PRP screening after the ToC.

## Rate each dimension separately

| Dimension | Strong | Conditional | Weak / Not evidenced / Unknown |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Formal call alignment | Read operative clause directly addresses the stated need/contribution in the applicable field/action | Plausible relation but design, sector or baseline confirmation is missing | Weak is peripheral; Not evidenced means reviewed text does not support the proposed relation; Unknown means relevant text/input is missing |
| European need | Relevant comparable evidence or an explicit policy diagnosis supports the need | Evidence covers another population/context, or local applicability needs checking | Do not invent local need or treat lack of data as no need |
| Policy momentum | Relevant adoption or concrete implementation/follow-through is documented | Proposal, consultation or agenda signal is relevant but outcome remains open | Newness alone is weak; unresolved status is Unknown |
| Possible contribution | A concrete plausible contribution can be traced to the need and recommendation | Contribution is a design option needing scientific/local validation | A keyword match alone is weak; no solution yet can remain Unknown |

Use English labels **Strong**, **Conditional**, **Weak**, **Not evidenced**, **Unknown** with reasons and a next check. Record evidence confidence separately as High / Medium / Low, explaining authority, read coverage and uncertainty. A clearly verified mismatch can have High confidence; low confidence does not mean poor project quality. Labels are internal diagnostic judgments, not official assessment categories, GRADE or evaluator points.

For a future-year baseline, mark formal fit explicitly provisional even when the earlier-year clause matches strongly. Missing country means agency emphasis Unknown, not absent. Do not require a validated solution to recognise a relevant need; distinguish need-level fit from intervention-level fit. Do not calculate a combined score or optimise priority count.

## Recommend opportunities, not a completed project

Select normally two to four relevant opportunities where evidence supports them; fewer is valid. For each, link policy need/recommendation, source/date/status, connection to the account, possible contribution, conditions/limits and question for Joos/project owner. Add substantive contradictions and poor matches when they affect choice.

Keep “the policy requests X”, “the intake reports Y”, “the research suggests Z” and “we could investigate contribution W” distinct. European cooperation added value remains a design question if not described. Do not attach inclusion, digital or green labels only to increase apparent relevance. Leave final selection, ToC, partners and PRP work to the later phases.
